ECU-plug resistance tests
Readings pending

Procedure logged 24 August 2026, ahead of readings · WSM p.177–179, tests 9, 10, 11, 12 · diagrams RR1825E, RR1826E, RR1827E

Common setup. Ignition off. ECU multi-plug disconnected (under the passenger seat). Multimeter on ohms. Probe the harness side; sensors stay connected at the engine end. Wiggle the loom during every reading: a value that jumps indicates an intermittent fault. Directly relevant, as the water event was on that side of the vehicle.

Test 1 · Coolant temperatureThermistor, pins 7 to 25 (p.179, test 12). 5700–5900 Ω at 0 °C · 2400–2600 at 20 °C · 1100–1300 at 40 °C · 500–700 at 60 °C · 300–400 at 80 °C. Expect c. 1500–2200 Ω at Houston garage temperature. Tests sensor and harness together.Shorted or low: the ECU reads warm, giving a lean cranking mixture, which can plausibly cause the no-start. Open: reads very cold, toward enrichment and flooding; does not fit the present symptoms.
Test 2 · Fuel temperatureThermistor, pins 32 to 25 (p.178, test 11). Same resistance table. The sensor sits in the fuel rail and is a possible casualty of the professional rail cleaning if not removed first.Trim authority only: a failed FTS cannot alone cause a no-start. Log a bad reading; do not treat it as the answer.
Test 3 · Injector banksPin 13 = left bank (1, 3, 5, 7), p.177, test 9 · pin 11 = right bank (2, 4, 6, 8), p.178, test 10. Measure from the bank pin to the shared injector +12V supply node, not pin to pin. Per bank: 4–5 Ω good · 5–6 Ω one injector or wire open · 8–9 Ω two open · 16–17 Ω three open · ∞ bank dead.Complements the noid: the noid proved the pulse is commanded; this proves the current path through every coil.

Traps, before probing

  • Do not meter pin 11 to pin 13. That reads both banks in series through the supply node (c. 8 Ω) and mimics the “two injectors open” value, a false diagnosis.
  • Supply-node access with ignition off (main relay open, so battery positive does not reach it): either (a) pull the main relay and probe its socket terminal 87, or (b) unplug one injector and probe the supply-side terminal of its harness plug. Unplug a right-bank injector when measuring the left bank, and the reverse: pulling one from the measured bank removes a parallel coil and reads c. 5.3 Ω, mimicking “one open”.

Sequencing. These three tests and the both-banks noid (done) clear the whole sensor and injector electrical picture from one seat. The rotor and wire-order check at No. 1 TDC (checklist item 2) remains the prime suspect and the final step.

Distributor mechanics

Drive gearDriven by a skew (helical) gear off the camshaft, so the shaft rotates (c. 30°, clockwise going in) as it drops into mesh. Lifted far enough to clear mesh, it can re-engage one tooth off: c. 26–28° at the distributor, c. 52–55° at the crank. Not a multiple of the 45° cap-terminal spacing, so a tooth jump is a half-position error that moving plug wires can never correct. The shaft's lower end drives the oil pump (post-1976 female drive); after any shaft lift, confirm full seating and oil pressure on restart.
AdvanceCentrifugal: weights and two different springs (primary tight; secondary with the slack, elongated loop; not interchangeable) under the pickup plate. The upper shaft (rotor carrier and reluctor) rides on the advance posts and on most units can be reassembled 180° out, exactly four cap positions. A missing or unseated spring lets the mechanism fall to full advance at cranking speed, giving starter kickback and spitting.
Factory refitSet No. 1 to 6° BTDC on compression; hold the rotor c. 30° counter-clockwise of the No. 1 cap terminal; drop the distributor in, and gear mesh rotates it clockwise onto the terminal. Verify the rotor points at No. 1, clamp, then strobe-time.
Decision treeTDC No. 1 on compression, pull the cap, read the rotor. (a) At the No. 1 terminal: phasing correct, timing not the fault; test the spark path (prime suspect the old shorted rotor as the original no-start; fit the new one and re-test). (b) Between two terminals: gear tooth jump; pull the distributor and re-seat per the factory procedure. (c) c. 180° away, four terminals: advance or upper-shaft mis-clock; re-clock the internals, or move all 8 wires four positions as a get-home measure only. (d) Exactly one terminal off: moving the wires one position works, though no plausible failure mode here produces it.All specifications for this procedure (firing order, numbering, rotation, timing, air gap, 5/7 cross-fire routing) are in the verified ignition reference above.

The owner's kit had no fitting that mates the rail because there is nothing to mate. The 13CU fuel rail has no Schrader valve, test port or service valve; the whole WSM was searched, and “Schrader” appears only in the A/C section (p.632–670), never in the fuel section. The factory test breaks into the feed line: depressurise (pull the pump relay, crank to stall, ignition off), disconnect the feed line between the fuel rail and the filter, adjacent to the filter, and connect the gauge in line there (Test 18, p.183; revised Test 20/10, p.218–219).

  • Special tool: Land Rover fuel-pressure gauge 18G 1500 (OCR reads “18C 1500”, p.219), a gauge and hose that splices into the same feed line; not a rail adapter.
  • The revised procedure widens the specification slightly to 2.39–2.67 kgf/cm², 34–38 psi (p.219).
  • Rail union is the “nut and ferrule” type (p.198), a compression or flare union with no threaded test boss. The manual publishes no rail-union thread size and no feed or return hose bore, in neither the §04 specification data nor §19; absent, and not to be invented.
  • Filter unions torque 20–25 lbf·ft, 27–34 Nm (p.243).

Conclusion. With no rail test port, the owner's barbed-T splice into the feed line is the correct, factory-equivalent method (18G 1500 does the same). Keep it in the feed line adjacent to the filter or at the rail end of the same run; SAE 30R9 hose and proper clamps; depressurise before every opening and closing.
