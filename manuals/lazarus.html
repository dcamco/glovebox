Because the ignition is entirely aftermarket, there is no factory Land Rover crank sensor and no Land Rover part number for it, and the engine throws no OBD trouble codes. The Megajolt tune and map file should be backed up (ask Noah whether laptop files or paperwork came with the truck); it is effectively irreplaceable.

Crank Sensor & No-Start Diagnosis

The suspected “crank sensor” is the variable-reluctance (VR) sensor reading the 36−1 wheel: a generic Ford EDIS-type pickup, not a Land Rover sensor.

Type
Two-wire variable-reluctance (magnetic) sensor. Polarised: with the two wires reversed, EDIS produces no spark.
Bracket
Trigger Wheels Rover V8 adjustable bracket, P/N RV8-VR-Bkt; may use 5 mm spacers.
Sensor donor
A Ford EDIS VR sensor. The kits are “tested with both Ford and GM VR pickups”; Ford OEM recommended. Common donors: Ford CVH / Zetec crank sensor (Escort, Fiesta, Mondeo, Focus era) or a 1999–2001 Miata CKP; effectively any Ford VR sensor of the correct body.
Do not order blind

Inspect the truck first. Locate the two-wire sensor aimed at the toothed wheel on the front of the crank, read its casting and label numbers, then buy the exact match or the cross-referenced Ford sensor. Builds differ; the markings on the installed sensor are the source of truth.

Sensor Test, Bench or On-Car

Wiring / connectorInspect lead for heat or chafe damage; check shield and groundCommon failure near the hot engine

No-Start / Stall Sequence

Engine cranks but will not fire, or cuts out at speed:

  1. No spark at the coils. Suspect the crank (VR) signal chain, in order: VR sensor resistance and AC output; sensor air gap to trigger wheel; sensor wiring, connector and polarity; EDIS module power and ground; Megajolt power, ground and configuration.
  2. Spark present, no start. Fuel side: pump, pressure, filter, injectors, ECU temperature and O2 inputs (the builder’s standard EFI checklist).
  3. Note. If the Megajolt controller fails but the crank signal is good, EDIS defaults to a fixed ~10° BTDC and the engine still runs. A true crank no-start therefore points firmly at the VR sensor chain.

VR and EDIS specifications from the Trigger Wheels Rover V8 product line and the Autosport Labs MegaJolt / EDIS installation guides; ignition layout from the builder’s forum threads.

Diagnosis

Active investigation: hot no-start and hot stall. Open and unresolved.

Hot No-Start
Open · Unresolved

Working theory, not a verdict

Root cause is not confirmed until the hot spark test, or a live signal measurement, is performed. The entry is not to be closed, and parts are not to be replaced on assumption alone.

Last update: investigation in progress; no on-truck electrical test yet completed.

Symptoms, as Observed by the Owner

  • Hard starting when hot, progressively worse. Cranks at length before catching; the required cranking time has grown steadily.
  • Cold starts perfect, every time. The fault is heat-gated.
  • Will not hold idle when hot. Dies the moment the throttle closes.
  • Stays running if RPM is held. Driven home two-footing the throttle; on lifting off at the destination it died and would not restart.
  • Recovers after ~1–2 min standing, then fires. Continued idling never recovers it; a short cool-down does.

Leading Hypothesis, Unconfirmed

Heat-marginal crank-signal amplitude in the VR → EDIS chain. A VR sensor’s output voltage rises with RPM against the EDIS module’s fixed trigger threshold. This accounts for every symptom: death at idle, rescue by revs, long hot cranking (lowest RPM, weakest signal), progressive worsening (a degrading part), and recovery after a short cool-down (a marginal junction dropping below its failure temperature).

Fuel: down-ranked, not being pursued

Rescue by throttle is the reverse of fuel starvation; a weak pump struggles most under load, not least. A perfect cold start and clean recovery after 1–2 min also argue against pump or rail heat-soak. Fuel is retained only as a possible separate contributor to a rough hot idle, not as the no-start cause.

Suspects, Ranked

All heat-gated, all in the same signal chain.

RankSuspectRationaleCost
1. VR crank sensorWeak amplitudeBest fit to the RPM-dependent rescue; classic heat-marginal winding or junction~£24 Trigger Wheels p25; ~$20–40 cross-reference
2. VR connector, wiring, groundResistive jointAttenuates the signal identically and goes intermittent with heat. Connector noted as “a bit oily”.Nil to minor
3. Air gapSanity check onlyRanked down: a static gap error would affect cold starts too. Only thermal drift could make it heat-gated.Nil
4. EDIS-8 moduleHeat failureFails with heat, but tends to cut regardless of RPM; the clean rescue by revs argues against it~$20–50 Salvage

Tests Outstanding, in Order

  1. Hot spark test, the deciding test. When it dies hot, check for spark while cranking. No spark hot confirms the ignition / signal chain; spark present turns the investigation to fuel and the 14CUX.Not done
  2. VR signal at the sensor against the EDIS module input. Good at sensor, weak at module: wiring or connection. Weak at both: the sensor.Not done
  3. Voltage-drop test on the EDIS / Megajolt ground and 12V feed, hot and cranking. More than ~0.1–0.2 V on the ground leg indicates a bad ground.Not done
  4. Heat-gun resistance test of the removed sensor: cold ≈ 300–1,400 Ω, then heated. Resistance climbing toward open confirms a bad sensor.Not done
  5. Freeze-spray isolation. With the engine down, chill only the sensor, then only the module; whichever revives it is the culprit.Not done
  6. Monitor RPM in the Megajolt software (free) as it dies. Ragged or dropping out: upstream signal. Clean and steady: output / SAW side.Not done
  7. Clean the sensor tip (inspect the magnet for ferrous debris) and connector pins; trace the oil source, likely a front crank seal or timing-cover leak slung by the trigger wheel. Oil alone is unlikely to be the heat-gated root cause.Not done
