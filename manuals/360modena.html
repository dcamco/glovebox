Implications

  • Value.   • Parts. Mechanically identical to a US 360. A handful of emissions, lighting and alarm parts differ.

Heat exchanger · 10 August 2026
Decision

Oil/coolant heat exchanger: replace during the H&S major, or monitor

The present exchanger was fitted in January 2010 at 16,356 mi (RO 31305, $6,632), after the 2008 leak and the 2010 full failure with oil in coolant. It is now some 16 years old; its ~7.2k mi are irrelevant, corrosion being driven by age and chemistry. The brand and revision fitted by the Harlingen independent are unknown; the markings on the installed unit are the source of truth, and H&S are to read and photograph them.

Location
On the 360 the exchanger (PN 180846, shared with the 355) is coolant to gearbox oil, and bolts between the water-pump housing and the water outlet manifold; not beneath the intake plenum, which is 430-era lore. Access is the belt-service access (seats and bulkhead, water pump off): approximately 80% labour overlap with the paid H&S major.
Cost
Bundled with the major, approximately +$1.5–2k: part approximately $850–1,100, a few hours, and seals (sleeves 187173 ×2, O-ring 101044, crush washers 10263460 ×2). Standalone later, approximately $4k, the access being bought again. The next inexpensive window is the next belt service, about 2031.
Parts
OEM 180846, approximately $833 from Ferrari Parts Exchange; also Ricambi, Eurospares, Scuderia. Or the AW Italian upgraded full-copper kit 180846KIT: 70 tubes against 55, with couplers, clamps and gaskets; price on application, 800-631-5589. Hill Engineering does not make one; their range is bearings and flywheels.
Decision gate
If the H&S major is still open: add it. A keeper car, a 16-year-old unit, a proven failure mode on this chassis, and the water pump off regardless. If the major is already closed up: defer and monitor. Expansion-tank sludge check each month the car is driven; gearbox oil inspected or sent for analysis (glycol markers) at each change; yellow-metal-safe gear oil only; coolant every two years. Do not pay $4k standalone on a system that tests clean.

Intelligence · Eric Goodkind, July 2026

Ferrari master technician of 20 years, Leander, Texas; Modena Lucerna installer. Bring a Trailer handle Converted_4re; his public comments match his private quotes verbatim.

Kits
Modena Lucerna is the most OEM-complete: two pedals, clutch-position sensor and bracket, heat shielding, speed-sensor plug, hard lines for pedal feel. Dr. S / Cambio Manuale also excellent. Avoid EAG and MMX: crude machining tolerances, rough engagement, less complete, in his direct experience.
ECU
360Trev (Senica.ga tuning), which flashes the car to be genuinely manual. Avoid piggyback recalibration methods (“nothing but issues”).
Method
His workshop converts only with the gearbox out, by the hydraulic (brake-fluid) method, renewing the throwout bearing or its internal seals every time as a safety item. The F1-fluid, transmission-in-place shortcut is refused. The conversion is therefore the clutch job: the same transmission-out event. Never do a standalone clutch with a conversion in prospect.
Cost
Workshop conversion typically $25–30k all-in. A DIY conversion adds the kit (price to be confirmed), 360Trev calibration, clutch, throwout bearing, fluids, special tools, gaskets and hardware, and possibly the rear main seal and flywheel. Their installation guide, $500: 500 pages, written for the 430 but “almost identical”, with 30 days of telephone and FaceTime support.
European spec
No difference for the conversion.
Clutch %
A snapshot of “80%” can mean 80% remaining or 80% worn, depending on the scanner; clutches slip at 85–90% worn. The seller’s “~73%” is ambiguous in direction, to be resolved at the H&S SD3/Leonardo read.
F1 parts
Keep them with the car. The market is flooded with used F1 pumps and actuators, and reversibility is worth more at resale than the parts would fetch. Nothing is permanently modified in the conversion.

Clutch Sourcing · research 27 July 2026

For this car (a street keeper, under 1,000 mi a year, standard power, not tracked): an OEM-specification single-plate replacement. Do not uprate. The clutch is replaced at the conversion, a transmission-out event, and the choice of kit, not of clutch, drives the decision.

  • The 360 clutch is a single plate, not twin, correcting a common assumption. Genuine Ferrari complete unit: PN 201680 (made by Valeo; ~215mm organic disc and pressure plate). The same disc serves F1 and gated cars; the difference lies in the release (throwout) bearing and hydraulics, not the friction disc. Converting to gated does not require a different disc.
  • Uprated clutches (Kevlar or sintered, higher clamp load) are the wrong choice here: harsher engagement, grabbier take-up, faster flywheel wear, a cost paid for track or high-power use this car will not see. Standard power and light duty make OEM organic the smoother and cheaper choice. The original clutch’s rapid wear (58% in 8,544 mi, RO 26039) was caused by a failing throwout bearing, ailing F1 hydraulics and the hard early map, not by an under-specified clutch. The CS TCU on the modification plan already softens the map for the next clutch.
  • Throwout-bearing quality does matter; it was the original failure mode (RO 26039 line 08). Use the Hill Engineering uprated release (slave) bearing, CRB-360/430-MS for gated cars or CRB-360/430-F1S for F1: billet, higher-tensile aluminium, the established remedy for the OEM bearing’s weakness. Approximately $570–640. The one place to spend more.

Coordinate with the conversion kit

The F1-to-gated kits do not include the clutch disc or pressure plate; they reuse the existing clutch, or a fresh 201680 is supplied. What they do specify is the throwout bearing and the hydraulic conversion (brake-fluid method). Cambio Manuale (Modena Lucerna family) sells a Hill throwout bearing with brake-fluid seals for ~$800 as a kit option, and treats its replacement as a safety item, matching Eric’s method. The EAG kit (~$21k) bundles “hydraulic clutch components” but no named clutch. GTE’s kit is the outlier, specifying a twin-disc F430-based clutch: a genuine hardware change, not for a standard-power street car.

Choose the kit first (Modena Lucerna or Cambio Manuale, per Eric), then buy the OEM 201680 and the kit’s specified Hill throwout bearing together at conversion. A clutch bought now is money on a shelf while the SD3 read may show years of life left in the present one. Do not buy in advance.

Clutch-Wear Reconciliation · 24 July 2026

Interpreting Mario’s “~73%”. The present clutch was fitted January 2006 at 8,544 mi (RO 26039); the car now shows 23,529 mi, so approximately 14,985 mi and some 20 years on this clutch.

(A), 73% remaining, is internally consistent. The original clutch’s 6.8% per 1k was not a clean signal of the map. It was co-caused by the failing throwout bearing and the slow, failing F1 pump (RO 26039 lines 01 and 08): ailing hydraulics mean long slip on every engagement, accelerating wear on top of the hard early map. The replacement clutch has run on healthy hydraulics since (F1 pump replaced 2006 and 2009), so a rate three to four times slower is what would be expected even on the same early map. The usage supports it: under 800 mi a year (~15k over ~20 years), a garaged car spared the stop-start, hill-start and traffic duty the early F1 map punishes most. Low-abuse miles and healthy hydraulics make ~1.8% per 1k credible.

(B), 73% worn, implies ~4.9% per 1k: wear continuing at ~72% of the original, ailing-hydraulics rate despite healthy hydraulics. That would mean the hard map alone does most of the damage, and the clutch is near replacement (clutches slip at ~85–90% worn; 73% worn means planning the job, with roughly 2–3k mi of margin at this rate). Not impossible, but it requires the map to be nearly as damaging as a failing pump, which the low mileage and absence of any slip complaint argue against.

Reading: (A), 73% remaining, is the more likely, and not on the physics alone. A seller quoting a figure as a selling point quotes it in the flattering direction; “73%” sells the car only if it means 73% of life left. Were the honest figure 73% worn, a seller would talk around it, not towards it. The physics and the seller’s incentive point the same way.

Threshold

A concern only if the SD3 read returns ≥ ~75–80% worn, near the 85–90% slip threshold. The clutch would then be an immediate item, and the conversion-or-clutch decision would trigger at once, a transmission-out clutch job being the conversion event. Direction and true % to be resolved at the H&S SD3/Leonardo read; the paper cannot answer it.
